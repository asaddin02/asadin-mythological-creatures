
=== sphinx (lengkap-informasi, batch-032)
Sphinx | tier rich | hybrid | jenis makhluk campuran | greek-mythology
Ringkas: A sphinx is a mythical creature with a lion's body and usually a human head, known as a tomb guardian in Egypt and as the riddler of Thebes in Greek myth.
Kemampuan: null — The Sphinx put a riddle to the Thebans and devoured those who failed to answer. [sphinx-c13, sphinx-c06, sphinx-c14]
Kemampuan: null — Sphinx statues guarded tombs against disturbance, and large sculpted sphinxes shaped like winged bulls stood outside palaces to guard against evil forces. [sphinx-c05, sphinx-c21]
sphinx-c01 (traditional-belief): A sphinx is a mythical creature with a lion's body, usually a human head, and sometimes wings.
sphinx-c02 (historical-record): The sphinx motif originated in Egypt and was known across the eastern Mediterranean in the Bronze Age.
sphinx-c03 (historical-record): Greek art portrayed the sphinx as a winged lioness with a woman's head.
sphinx-c04 (traditional-belief): The Greek sphinx has a lion's body, woman's upper body, eagle wings, and sometimes a serpent tail.
sphinx-c05 (historical-record): Sphinx statues guarded tombs against disturbance.
sphinx-c06 (traditional-belief): In the Theban myth, the sphinx devoured those who failed her riddle.
sphinx-c07 (traditional-belief): Oedipus answered 'human' and the sphinx leapt to her death.
sphinx-c08 (traditional-belief): The sphinx's riddle asks what has one voice and four, two, then three feet.
sphinx-c09 (traditional-belief): According to Theoi, the gods sent the Sphinx to plague the town of Thebes as punishment for some ancient crime; she preyed on its youths and devoured all who failed to solve her riddle.
sphinx-c10 (historical-record): In archaic vase paintings, sphinxes often appear among a procession of animals and fabulous creatures such as lions and bird-bodied sirens.
sphinx-c11 (traditional-belief): In the quotation from Hesiod's Theogony on Theoi, Khimaira, in love with Orthos, mothered the deadly Sphinx, the bane of the Kadmeians (Thebans).
sphinx-c12 (traditional-belief): According to Apollodorus, Hera sent the Sphinx, whose mother was Echidna and father Typhon; she had a woman's face, a lion's breast, feet, and tail, and a bird's wings.
sphinx-c13 (traditional-belief): Having learned a riddle from the Muses, the Sphinx sat on Mount Phicium and put it to the Thebans.
sphinx-c14 (traditional-belief): The Thebans often met to discuss the answer, and when they could not find it the Sphinx snatched one of them and gobbled him up.
sphinx-c15 (traditional-belief): Oedipus found the solution: the riddle referred to man, who as a babe goes on four limbs, as an adult on two feet, and as an old man gets a third support in a staff.
sphinx-c16 (traditional-belief): According to Hesiod's Theogony, the sphinx was born from the Chimaera, sister of the Nemean lion and half-sister of Kerberos.
sphinx-c17 (traditional-belief): The sphinx brought drought and famine and would leave the Thebans alone only if they solved her riddle.
sphinx-c18 (historical-record): Sphinxes were first created by the Egyptians and usually wore a nemes (head-dress) like those worn by pharaohs.
sphinx-c19 (historical-record): In the reign of Chephren, sphinxes became more widespread and were usually placed as guards outside or beside a mortuary temple, tomb, or funerary monument.
sphinx-c20 (traditional-belief): There is a story that Thutmose IV, still a prince, fell asleep in the shadow of the Sphinx and dreamt that it promised he would become king if he cleared the sand around the statue's feet.
sphinx-c21 (historical-record): Large sculpted sphinxes in the shape of winged bulls often stood in pairs outside palaces and guarded against evil forces.
sphinx-c22 (historical-record): One of the most famous sphinxes was found at Delphi; it originally sat on a 10 m Ionic column and was dedicated to the oracle by the Naxians around 560 BCE.
sphinx-c23 (historical-record): Sphinxes were also present in the art of the Minoan and Mycenaean cultures from the early second millennium BC.
sphinx-c24 (etymology): The word sphinx comes from the Greek Σφίγξ, associated by folk etymology with the verb σφίγγω (sphíngō), meaning "to squeeze" or "to tighten up".
sphinx-c25 (scholarly-interpretation): According to Hesiod, the Sphinx was a daughter of Orthrus and an unknown female (the Chimera, Echidna, or Ceto); according to Apollodorus and Lasus, a daughter of Echidna and Typhon.
sphinx-c26 (historical-record): The sphinx was the emblem of the ancient city-state of Chios and appeared on seals and the obverse of coins from the 6th century BC until the 3rd century AD.
sphinx-c27 (scholarly-interpretation): The exact riddle asked by the Sphinx was not specified by early tellers of the myth and was standardized only late in Greek history.
sphinx-c28 (traditional-belief): In late lore, Hera or Ares sent the Sphinx from her Aethiopian homeland to Thebes in Greece; the Greeks always remembered her foreign origin.
sphinx-c29 (traditional-belief): Bested at last, the Sphinx threw herself from her high rock and died.
sphinx-c30 (scholarly-interpretation): In South India the "sphinx" is known as puruṣamr̥ga (Sanskrit) or purushamirugam (Tamil), "human-animal", and is depicted in sculpture in temples and palaces with an apotropaic purpose.
sphinx-c31 (scholarly-interpretation): In Burma (Myanmar), the sphinx-like statue with a human head and two lion hindquarters is known as Manussiha; it is placed on the corners of Buddhist stupas, and its legends say Buddhist monks created it to protect a newborn royal baby from ogresses.
sphinx-c32 (scholarly-interpretation): Nora Nair, Norasingha, and Thep Norasingha are three names of the "sphinx" in Thailand; they are depicted walking upright, with the lower body of a lion or deer and the upper body of a human.
sphinx-c33 (scholarly-interpretation): Although the date of construction is uncertain, the consensus among Egyptologists is that the head of the Great Sphinx bears the likeness of the pharaoh Khafre, dating it to between 2600 and 2500 BC.
sphinx-c34 (scholarly-interpretation): The date of the first sphinx is unknown and the Great Sphinx of Giza has not been precisely dated; some scholars date it as far back as the reign of Cheops, ca 2500 BCE.
sphinx-c35 (scholarly-interpretation): In contrast to the Egyptians, the ancient Greeks saw the sphinx as a more troublesome creature.
sphinx-c36 (historical-record): From the 6th century BCE the Greek sphinx appeared in stone sculpture, sometimes with raised hind quarters.
sphinx-c37 (etymology): In the Boeotian dialect the name was phix, whence the name of the Boeotian mountain Phikion oros.
sphinx-c38 (scholarly-interpretation): The historian Susan Wise Bauer suggests the word "sphinx" was instead a Greek corruption of the Egyptian name "shesepankh", meaning "living image".

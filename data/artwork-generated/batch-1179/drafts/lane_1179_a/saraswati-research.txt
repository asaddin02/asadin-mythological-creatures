
=== saraswati (lengkap-informasi, batch-031)
Saraswati | tier rich | deity | jenis dewa | tradition-hindu
Ringkas: Saraswati is a Hindu goddess associated with learning, wisdom, music, and the arts; she is also the goddess of speech and, originally, a sacred river.
Kemampuan: null — The vina recalls Saraswati's gift of music to humanity; she is also called the inventor of Sanskrit and gives Ganesha pen and ink. [saraswati-c05, saraswati-c03]
Kemampuan: null — A Rigveda hymn says she bursts the ridges of the hills with her strong waves and discovers rivers for the tribes of men. [saraswati-c25, saraswati-c26]
Kemampuan: null — In a Rigveda hymn, Sarasvati casts down those who scorned the gods. [saraswati-c27]
Kemampuan: null — Saraswati cursed Brahma never to be worshipped by humanity. [saraswati-c19]
saraswati-c01 (religious-tradition): Saraswati is the Hindu goddess of learning, wisdom, music, and aesthetics.
saraswati-c02 (religious-tradition): As Vac, she is the goddess of speech.
saraswati-c03 (historical-record): Saraswati first appears in the Rigveda; in later religious texts she is identified as the inventor of Sanskrit and gives Ganesha the gifts of pen and ink.
saraswati-c04 (religious-tradition): Saraswati Puja is held on the first day of spring.
saraswati-c05 (historical-record): Saraswati is often depicted holding a vina.
saraswati-c06 (scholarly-interpretation): Her vina, manuscript, and rosary symbolize music, learning, and ritual.
saraswati-c07 (historical-record): Saraswati is conventionally accompanied by a swan, though this print shows a peacock.
saraswati-c08 (scholarly-interpretation): Saraswati was associated with a lost river of the same name and later with all waters.
saraswati-c09 (religious-tradition): She is also known as Bharati (eloquence), Shatarupa (existence), Vedamata ('mother of the Vedas'), Brahmi, Sarada, Vagisvari, and Putkari.
saraswati-c10 (religious-tradition): She is also a patron of the arts and sciences and the wife of Brahma, although the Bengal Vaisnavas considered her first the wife of Vishnu.
saraswati-c11 (etymology): Saraswati's name means 'elegant', 'flowing', and 'watery', which indicates her status as one of the early Aryan boundary rivers.
saraswati-c12 (religious-tradition): The Saraswati River (modern name Sarsuti), like the Ganges, flows from the Himalaya and is considered a sacred source of purification, fertility, and good fortune for those who bathe in it; the sacred river then developed into a personified deity.
saraswati-c13 (religious-tradition): During the festival worshippers wear yellow, which is associated with wisdom and prosperity; statues of the goddess are draped in yellow silk, and believers pray for blessings on their pens, books, and musical instruments.
saraswati-c14 (religious-tradition): During the festival children are taught to write for the first time, Brahmin priests are given fine food, and ancestors are venerated.
saraswati-c15 (religious-tradition): As the patron of music she is frequently prayed to by musicians before concerts, and as a goddess of intellectual pursuits by students before examinations.
saraswati-c16 (religious-tradition): Saraswati is also worshipped along with other major goddesses in the pan-Indian celebration of Navaratri.
saraswati-c17 (religious-tradition): In Hindu mythology Saraswati is the wife of the great god Brahma, but according to some traditions she was first the wife of Vishnu.
saraswati-c18 (religious-tradition): One day, while waiting for his wife to begin an important ceremony and unable to start the ritual on time, Brahma asked the gods' advice; they created a new wife for him, Gayatri, so that the ceremony could proceed at the proper time.
saraswati-c19 (religious-tradition): When Saraswati finally arrived and saw her husband with another woman she was displeased and cursed Brahma never to be worshipped by humanity (although he is worshipped today in parts of South-east Asia).
saraswati-c20 (religious-tradition): More specifically, Saraswati's son is the rishi (sage) Sarasvata, who, nourished by his mother's bountiful waters, withstood the Great Drought of Hindu mythology and survived as the definitive repository of the Vedas.
saraswati-c21 (historical-record): In Hindu art Saraswati is usually shown as a graceful youth with white skin, most often in a white sari (symbolizing purity) with a blue border.
saraswati-c22 (historical-record): Like many Hindu deities, Saraswati is often shown with four arms, each holding a symbolic object; in the left hands she can carry a palm-leaf manuscript and a ritual water vessel, and in the upper right hand she holds a white lotus.
saraswati-c23 (historical-record): She may also be shown riding her vehicle (vahana), either a swan or a goose.
saraswati-c24 (religious-tradition): Sarasvati is also worshipped as the goddess of learning in Jainism and by some Buddhist sects.
saraswati-c25 (religious-tradition): In a Rigveda hymn (Griffith's translation), Sarasvati with her might bursts the ridges of the hills with her strong waves, like one who digs for lotus stems.
saraswati-c26 (religious-tradition): The same Rigveda hymn praises Sarasvati for discovering rivers for the tribes of men and, rich in wealth, making poison flow away from them.
saraswati-c27 (religious-tradition): In the hymn, Sarasvati is said to cast down those who scorned the Gods, the brood of every Brsaya skilled in magic arts.
saraswati-c28 (religious-tradition): In the hymn, Sarasvati is called seven-sistered, sprung from a threefold source, and the prosperer of the Five Tribes.
saraswati-c29 (religious-tradition): Although one of the most important Hindu gods, Brahma is rarely actively worshipped; in mythology this is explained as the result of a curse from Saraswati.

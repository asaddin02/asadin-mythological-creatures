
=== heracles (lengkap-informasi, batch-032)

Ringkas: Heracles is a Greek mythic hero famed for his strength, his twelve labors, and his rise to godhood.

heracles-c01 (traditional-belief): Heracles is a Greek mythic hero renowned for strength and endurance.
heracles-c02 (traditional-belief): Zeus is named as Heracles's father and Alkmene as his mother.
heracles-c03 (traditional-belief): Hera sent two snakes to the infant Heracles, who strangled them.
heracles-c04 (traditional-belief): The Delphic Oracle ordered Heracles to perform twelve labors for Eurystheus.
heracles-c05 (traditional-belief): Diodorus says Heracles was commanded to complete twelve labors and promised immortality afterward.
heracles-c06 (traditional-belief): Heracles strangled the Nemean Lion and wore its pelt.
heracles-c07 (historical-record): Heracles was worshipped as a god throughout classical Greece.
heracles-c08 (traditional-belief): Hera drove Heracles insane so that he killed his wife and children.
heracles-c09 (traditional-belief): With the help of his nephew Iolaos, who used fire to stop the heads regrowing, Heracles killed the Hydra and dipped his arrows in its poisonous blood.
heracles-c10 (traditional-belief): On his journey in Hades, Heracles persuades the god Hades to let him take Kerberos provided he does so without weapons.
heracles-c11 (traditional-belief): Heracles shot down the eagle with one of his arrows and freed Prometheus.
heracles-c12 (traditional-belief): Athena took Heracles from the flames to Mount Olympus, where he married the goddess Hebe, received eternal youth, and was allowed to live with the gods forever.
heracles-c13 (historical-record): In Archaic and Classical Greek pottery, Heracles is often shown carrying a knotted club and a quiver of arrows and wearing a lion pelt, sometimes with a lion-head helmet.
heracles-c14 (traditional-belief): On his journey to that island in the western ocean, he set markers in the Strait of Gades that became known as the Pillars of Hercules.
heracles-c15 (traditional-belief): According to Apollodorus, Heracles received a sword from Hermes, a bow and arrows from Apollo, a golden breastplate from Hephaestus, and a robe from Athena; he had cut his club himself at Nemea.
heracles-c16 (traditional-belief): Antaeus, a Libyan giant who drew strength from the earth, challenged Heracles to a deadly wrestling match but was defeated when Heracles lifted him from the earth and crushed him.
heracles-c17 (traditional-belief): Heracles defeated Thanatos, the god of death, in a wrestling match when he came to fetch Alcestis to the Underworld.
heracles-c18 (etymology): The Greek name Ἡρακλῆς literally means "glory/fame of Hera".
heracles-c19 (historical-record): Heracles was thought to be both hero and god, possessing superhuman strength; no tomb was ever identified as his, and at some festivals sacrifice was offered to him first as a hero and then as a god.
heracles-c20 (traditional-belief): Deianeira smeared a cloak with the blood of Nessos and sent it to Heracles; when he wore it, the cloak burned his skin terribly.
heracles-c21 (historical-record): The earliest complete depiction of Heracles's twelve labours in ancient sculpture is on the metopes of the Temple of Zeus at Olympia (completed 457 BCE).
heracles-c22 (historical-record): Heracles was widely worshipped as a patron of young men and gymnasia.
heracles-c23 (traditional-belief): Cerberus, the hound of Hades who guarded the entrance to the underworld; Heracles was sent to fetch him as one of his twelve labours and captured him with the approval of the goddess Persephone.

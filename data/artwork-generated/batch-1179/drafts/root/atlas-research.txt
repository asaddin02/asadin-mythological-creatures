
=== atlas (lengkap-informasi, batch-037)
Atlas | tier rich | deity | jenis dewa | greek-mythology
Ringkas: Atlas is a Greek Titan whom Zeus condemned to hold up the heavens on his shoulders; he is also remembered as a teacher of astronomy and the namesake of a mountain range in North Africa.
Kemampuan: null — Atlas bears the heavens on his shoulders as punishment from Zeus; Aeschylus calls him pre-eminent in mighty strength. [atlas-c01, atlas-c13]
Kemampuan: null — Homer says Atlas knows the depths of all the seas, and he is also called the instructor of mankind in astronomy. [atlas-c12, atlas-c11]
atlas-c01 (traditional-belief): Atlas is a Greek Titan punished by Zeus to hold up the heavens.
atlas-c02 (traditional-belief): Atlas is a son of Iapetus and Clymene or Themis and brother of Prometheus.
atlas-c03 (traditional-belief): Atlas was punished for leading the Titans against the Olympian gods.
atlas-c04 (historical-record): Hesiod locates Atlas in the land of the Hesperides holding up the heavens.
atlas-c05 (traditional-belief): Hercules asked Atlas to fetch the Hesperides' apples while temporarily taking his burden.
atlas-c06 (traditional-belief): Hercules tricked Atlas into resuming his burden.
atlas-c07 (traditional-belief): Traditions say Atlas guarded heaven's pillars or held up the sky.
atlas-c08 (traditional-belief): In another account, Perseus turned Atlas into a mountain with Medusa's head.
atlas-c09 (historical-record): Hellenistic and Roman art depicts Atlas straining to hold a globe on his shoulders.
atlas-c10 (traditional-belief): Atlas was the Titan god who bore the sky aloft and personified the quality of endurance.
atlas-c11 (traditional-belief): Atlas was also the god who instructed mankind in astronomy, used by sailors for navigation and by farmers to measure the seasons.
atlas-c12 (historical-record): Homer's Odyssey says Atlas knows the depths of all the seas and guards (or holds) the tall pillars that keep sky and earth apart.
atlas-c13 (historical-record): Aeschylus's Prometheus Bound describes Atlas as pre-eminent in mighty strength, moaning as he supports the vault of heaven on his back.
atlas-c14 (historical-record): Pindar writes that Atlas still struggles to bear the weight of heaven far from his fathers' land, but almighty Zeus set the Titans free.
atlas-c15 (historical-record): According to Pseudo-Apollodorus, Heracles agreed but by a trick gave the sphere back to Atlas, asking him to take the sky while he put a cushion on his head.
atlas-c16 (historical-record): Pseudo-Apollodorus states the golden apples were not in Libya but with Atlas among the Hyperboreans.
atlas-c17 (historical-record): Pausanias describes Atlas on the chest of Cypselus supporting heaven and earth on his shoulders while carrying the apples of the Hesperides.
atlas-c18 (historical-record): In Ovid's Metamorphoses, the huge Atlas becomes a mountain: his beard and hair turn to forests, his shoulders to cliffs, his hands to ridges.
atlas-c19 (traditional-belief): Atlas's daughters were the star-nymphs the Pleiades and Hyades, and his son was the beautiful youth Hyas.
atlas-c20 (historical-record): Diodorus Siculus offers a rationalised reading: Atlas mastered the science of the stars and discovered their spherical arrangement, so he was believed to bear the whole firmament on his shoulders.
atlas-c21 (scholarly-interpretation): The name Atlas perhaps conveys the meaning 'suffering' or 'very enduring'.
atlas-c22 (traditional-belief): Atlas was the father of the nymph Calypso and the seven Pleiades.
atlas-c23 (historical-record): Later tradition, including Herodotus, associates Atlas with the Atlas Mountains in North Africa.
atlas-c24 (historical-record): In Greek art from the 6th century BCE, Atlas often appears in depictions of the labours of Hercules, most notably on a metope from the Temple of Zeus at Olympia (c. 460 BCE) where he stands in the gardens of the Hesperides.
atlas-c25 (historical-record): For Plato, Atlas was the eponymous first king of Atlantis; his name was also given to a huge mountain range in Northern Africa, the Atlantic Ocean and any large collection of maps.
atlas-c26 (scholarly-interpretation): The story of Perseus turning Atlas into a mountain may go back to the 5th century BCE.
atlas-c27 (historical-record): Telamon is a surname of Atlas describing him as the sufferer or bearer of heaven.

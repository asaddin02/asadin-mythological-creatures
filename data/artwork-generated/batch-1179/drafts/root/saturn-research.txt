
=== saturn (lengkap-informasi, batch-035)

saturn-c01 (religious-tradition): Saturnus is a Roman god associated with Greek Cronus.
saturn-c02 (religious-tradition): Saturn is an agricultural god often shown with a scythe.
saturn-c03 (traditional-belief): Roman legend places Saturn in Latium.
saturn-c04 (traditional-belief): Saturn ruled Latium during a prosperous and peaceful golden age.
saturn-c05 (traditional-belief): Saturn taught farming and viticulture.
saturn-c06 (historical-record): Saturn's temple at the Capitoline Hill housed the Roman treasury and Senate records.
saturn-c07 (historical-record): Saturnus, god of seed and sowing, was honored at Saturnalia on December 17.
saturn-c08 (historical-record): People exchanged candles or small figurines at Saturnalia.
saturn-c09 (historical-record): Saturnalia temporarily reversed the roles of slaves and masters.
saturn-c10 (scholarly-interpretation): Some authors believe Saturn existed in Roman mythology long before the 'invasion' of Greek religion and link him to the Etruscan god Satre, but this is entirely speculative.
saturn-c11 (traditional-belief): Cronus, the Greek figure associated with Saturn, was the son of Uranus and Gaea; after Zeus and his brothers defeated the Titans, Cronus was expelled from Mt. Olympus.
saturn-c12 (traditional-belief): Saturn's arrival was welcomed by the Roman god Janus, the two-faced god of beginnings and ends; he quickly established himself and even founded the nearby city of Saturnia.
saturn-c13 (traditional-belief): He also helped the locals rid themselves of their 'barbaric' customs and adopt a more civic and moral lifestyle.
saturn-c14 (historical-record): The temple fell into disrepair and was rebuilt during the reign of Emperor Augustus.
saturn-c15 (historical-record): The Saturnalia was celebrated in December from the 17th to the 23rd and was connected to the winter grain sowing.
saturn-c16 (historical-record): The Saturnalia immediately preceded the festival of Ops, Saturn's partner or consort and the goddess of the harvest, who became associated with the Greek goddess Rhea.
saturn-c17 (historical-record): The festival was presided over by a mock king, the King of Misrule or Saturnalicius princeps.
saturn-c18 (historical-record): The festival survived into the Christian era, taking on a new identity and the name Brumalia.
saturn-c19 (historical-record): Saturn was also linked to another ancient Italian deity, Lua.
saturn-c20 (historical-record): Saturn is remembered through Saturday and through the sixth planet from the sun, which bears his name.
saturn-c21 (historical-record): His temple, built around 498 BCE, stood at the foot of Capitoline Hill and housed the Roman treasury and the records and decrees of the Senate.
saturn-c22 (religious-tradition): Saturn was identified with Kronos and received sacrifice according to Greek ritual, with the head uncovered.
saturn-c23 (religious-tradition): The Temple of Saturn, the oldest temple recorded by the pontiffs, was dedicated on the Saturnalia, and the woolen bonds on the feet of its ivory cult statue were loosened that day to symbolize the god's liberation.
saturn-c24 (historical-record): After the sacrifice at the temple there was a public banquet and a lectisternium (an image of the god placed as if in attendance), which Livy says was introduced in 217 BC.
saturn-c25 (historical-record): The original day was given over to the Opalia, honoring Ops, who personified abundance and the fruits of the earth and was Saturn's consort.
saturn-c26 (historical-record): Augustus limited the holiday to three days so that the civil courts would not have to be closed longer than necessary.
saturn-c27 (historical-record): The Saturnalia was the most popular holiday of the Roman year.
saturn-c28 (historical-record): Saturn was described as a god of time, generation, dissolution, abundance, wealth, agriculture, periodic renewal and liberation.
saturn-c29 (traditional-belief): Saturn's consort was his sister Ops, with whom he fathered Jupiter, Neptune, Pluto, Juno, Ceres and Vesta.
saturn-c30 (historical-record): The Temple of Saturn in the Roman Forum housed the state treasury and archives (aerarium) of the Roman Republic and early Roman Empire.
saturn-c31 (etymology): According to Varro, Saturn's name derives from satus, meaning 'sowing'.
saturn-c32 (scholarly-interpretation): A perhaps more probable etymology links the name with the Etruscan god Satre and place names such as Satria, an ancient town of Latium, and Saturae palus, a marsh in Latium.
saturn-c33 (etymology): One interpretation holds that Saturn got his name because he was 'sated' with years.
saturn-c34 (historical-record): Saturn himself was represented as veiled (involutus), for example in a wall painting from Pompeii showing him holding a sickle and covered with a white veil.
saturn-c35 (scholarly-interpretation): In Roman and Etruscan reckoning Saturn is a wielder of lightning; no other agricultural god is one.
saturn-c36 (historical-record): The identification with Ba'al Hammon later gave rise to the African Saturn, a cult that enjoyed great popularity until the 4th century.
saturn-c37 (historical-record): The figure of Saturn, kept bound in wool during the year, was released from its bindings for the festival.
saturn-c38 (traditional-belief): There was also a tradition that Saturn was an immigrant god received by Janus after he was usurped by his son Jupiter and expelled from Greece.
saturn-c39 (traditional-belief): In the myth Saturn was the original, autochthonous ruler of the Capitolium, hence called the Mons Saturnius in older times, where the town of Saturnia once stood.
saturn-c40 (scholarly-interpretation): But Saturn also had a less benevolent aspect, as indicated by the blood shed in his honor during gladiatorial munera.
saturn-c41 (scholarly-interpretation): H. S. Versnel reads contradictions in the god, being a foreigner yet holding one of Rome's oldest sanctuaries and being a god of liberation kept in fetters for most of the year, as signs of his capacity to blur social distinctions.
saturn-c42 (scholarly-interpretation): The position of Saturn's festival in the Roman calendar led to his association with concepts of time, especially the transition of the New Year.
saturn-c43 (historical-record): According to Wikipedia, the temple was consecrated in 497 BC, while the area Saturni was built by king Tullus Hostilius, as confirmed by E. Gjerstad's archaeological studies.
saturn-c44 (historical-record): As early as Andronicus (3rd century BC), Jupiter was called the son of Saturn.
saturn-c45 (scholarly-interpretation): Some features of the god are certainly later and arrived after 217 BCE, the year in which the Greek customs of the Kronia were introduced into the Saturnalia.
saturn-c46 (traditional-belief): In Virgil's Aeneid (Dryden's translation), Saturn came, having fled the power of Jove, robbed of his realms and banished from above.
saturn-c47 (traditional-belief): Saturn brought the men dispersed on the hills into towns, ordained laws, taught civil customs, and called the land where he lay safe from his son Latium.
saturn-c48 (traditional-belief): With his mild empire came peace and plenty, and hence the golden times derived their name.
saturn-c49 (traditional-belief): In Virgil's description, godlike ancestors are placed in ranks; among them old Saturn with his crooked scythe on high, Italus, and ancient Janus with his double face.

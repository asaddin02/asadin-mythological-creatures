
=== hanuman (lengkap-informasi, batch-034)
Hanuman | tier rich | deity | jenis dewa | tradition-hindu
Ringkas: Hanuman is a monkey-bodied deity of Indian mythology, Rama's devoted companion known for strength, speed, shape-changing and devotion.
Kemampuan: flight — Hanuman flies across the ocean to find Sita and flies north at lightning speed to the Himalayas. [hanuman-c05, hanuman-c13]
Kemampuan: shapeshifting — Hanuman can take any form and increase or decrease his body size at will; in Griffith's translation he stands dilated in gigantic size before their eyes. [hanuman-c04, hanuman-c07, hanuman-c41]
Kemampuan: supernatural-strength — Hanuman has enormous strength and inherits might and speed from the Wind God; he uproots a whole mountain and Bhima cannot move his tail. [hanuman-c03, hanuman-c15, hanuman-c06, hanuman-c17]

hanuman-c01 (religious-tradition): Hanuman is an animal-formed figure in Indian mythology revered as a god.
hanuman-c02 (religious-tradition): Hanuman is a monkey-bodied deity, Rama's companion, and an exemplar of devotion.
hanuman-c03 (traditional-belief): Mythic texts describe Hanuman as the Wind God's monkey child with strength and Vedic learning.
hanuman-c04 (traditional-belief): Hanuman is devoted to Rama and can assume any form.
hanuman-c05 (traditional-belief): Hanuman flies over the sea to locate Sita and report back to Rama.
hanuman-c06 (traditional-belief): Unable to identify the healing herbs, Hanuman carries the whole mountain to the battlefield.
hanuman-c07 (traditional-belief): Hanuman can change form and alter his size.
hanuman-c08 (historical-record): Hanuman remains worshiped as a god in parts of India.
hanuman-c09 (religious-tradition): The Met notes Hanuman's veneration for humility and loyalty, with devotees seeking strength and courage.
hanuman-c10 (religious-tradition): Hanuman amulets are believed to protect their wearers and bring well-being.
hanuman-c11 (traditional-belief): While inside the demon fort searching for Sita, Hanuman sets the whole place on fire and warns Ravana of an impending attack unless Sita is returned unharmed.
hanuman-c12 (traditional-belief): In the Rama-Ravana battle Hanuman kills several demon generals and also brings Rama's brother back to life.
hanuman-c13 (traditional-belief): The battle rages in Lanka while the Himalayas are far to the north and the herbs are needed before dawn; Hanuman leaps into the air, flies north at lightning speed and alights atop the Himalayas.
hanuman-c14 (traditional-belief): The group of monkeys helped Rama build a bridge across the sea and cross with a monkey army that stormed the demon stronghold and freed Sita.
hanuman-c15 (traditional-belief): From his father, the Wind God, Hanuman inherits his might and speed.
hanuman-c16 (traditional-belief): In the epic Hanuman is described as having an extremely pleasant face, perfect diction, elegant speech and faultless manners, and as knowing the three Vedas.
hanuman-c17 (traditional-belief): In the Mahabharata, Bhima cannot move even a whisker of an old monkey's tail; the monkey turns out to be Hanuman, the mightiest being on earth.
hanuman-c18 (traditional-belief): As a mark of affection to Bhima, Hanuman agreed to reside on the flag of the chariot of Bhima's younger brother Arjuna.
hanuman-c19 (traditional-belief): Hanuman is the child of the Wind God and the princess Anjana; he is called by his mother's name, Anjaneya, as often as by his father's.
hanuman-c20 (religious-tradition): According to the Puranas, the couple prayed to Shiva for a son, and Hanuman was born from an aspect of Shiva through the agency of the Wind God.
hanuman-c21 (traditional-belief): Hanuman has two patronyms: Vayu-putra (Wind's son) and Kesari-nandan (Kesari's son).
hanuman-c22 (traditional-belief): Hanuman mistook the sun for a ripe fruit and leapt into the air to grab it.
hanuman-c23 (etymology): According to this story the thunderbolt hit its target, the monkey child's chin was disfigured, and he came to be known as Hanuman (hanu = chin; man = having), "he of the [broken] chin".
hanuman-c24 (traditional-belief): In this story Hanuman tells the king of the gods that he is unslayable, being born of Shiva.
hanuman-c25 (traditional-belief): The Puranas also say Surya, the Sun God, taught Hanuman the Vedas and all other branches of learning, which he learnt by trotting alongside the Sun's chariot.
hanuman-c26 (historical-record): In the 16th century CE the poet Tulsidas retold the Ramayana in Awadhi and composed several hymns, including the Hanuman Chalisa, still recited by thousands today.
hanuman-c27 (scholarly-interpretation): The canticle of about 40 verses celebrates the qualities Hanuman came to signify over the centuries: strength, devotion, celibacy and righteousness.
hanuman-c28 (religious-tradition): In the translated extract of the Hanuman Chalisa, demons and ghosts do not come near when they hear the mighty one's name, and diseases die and sorrows disappear for one who constantly chants Hanuman's name.
hanuman-c29 (historical-record): A small shrine to Hanuman is almost de rigueur in police stations and wrestling clubs across India, especially the north; outside India he is known in Hindu-influenced countries such as Indonesia, Thailand and Malaysia.
hanuman-c30 (historical-record): Hanuman is most often depicted with his favourite weapon, the mace, and often flying across the sky with one hand holding up the mountain of herbs and the other a mace.
hanuman-c31 (historical-record): Hanuman is a recurring figure in the Ramayana panels and motifs that adorn temples built over the centuries in India.
hanuman-c32 (religious-tradition): In Griffith's translation the Wind God promises that the child he gives will be strong, brave and wise, with untiring might and limbs that spring as his sire's do.
hanuman-c33 (religious-tradition): In Griffith's Ramayana the child leapt three hundred leagues above the ground after what seemed fruit.
hanuman-c34 (religious-tradition): Brahmá bestowed the boon that in the brunt of battle none should slay the Wind-God's son with steel.
hanuman-c35 (religious-tradition): Indra swore that the bolt he hurls from heaven should never injure him.
hanuman-c36 (religious-tradition): In Griffith's Ramayana Indra's bolt struck the child, who fell on a rock and had his cheek shattered.
hanuman-c37 (religious-tradition): In his speech Hanumán claims he can circle Meru a thousand times with airy leaps and stir the sea with his arms until its waters flee their bed.
hanuman-c38 (religious-tradition): Hanumán declares he can spring through the air far swifter than the feathered King.
hanuman-c39 (religious-tradition): In Griffith's Ramayana Hanumán musters his might and springs from the mountain height, uprooted trees following him.
hanuman-c40 (religious-tradition): The shadow of Hanumán's vast form on the ocean flew like a ship before the gale, and the sea beneath his course raged and swelled.
hanuman-c41 (religious-tradition): Hanumán, his soul stirred to vigorous effort, stood before their joyous eyes dilated in gigantic size.

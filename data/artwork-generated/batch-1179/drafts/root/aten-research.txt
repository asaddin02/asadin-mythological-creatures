
=== aten (lengkap-informasi, batch-038)
Aten | tier rich | deity | jenis dewa | egyptian-mythology
Ringkas: Aten is an ancient Egyptian sun god shown as a sun disk with rays ending in hands, worshipped as the principal deity under Akhenaten.
Kemampuan: null — His rays end in hands offering life, and the Great Hymn praises him as the one who gives breath to all he made. According to the hymn, beings live when he shines and die when he rests. [aten-c02, aten-c07, aten-c23]
Kemampuan: null — Atenism holds that Aten created all countries and people, and the Great Hymn says he moulds the earth to his wish alone. [aten-c31, aten-c20]
Kemampuan: elemental-control — According to the Great Hymn, Aten provides a Flood in the sky for foreigners and a Flood from the underworld for Egypt, and makes the seasons. [aten-c21, aten-c22]
Kemampuan: null — The Great Hymn describes Aten removing the darkness and granting his rays at daybreak, while his rays bind the lands to the limit of his creation. [aten-c18, aten-c16]
aten-c01 (religious-tradition): Aten is an Egyptian sun god represented as the sun disk.
aten-c02 (religious-tradition): Aten's rays end in hands that offer life to the king and women close to him.
aten-c03 (historical-record): During Akhenaten's reign, Aten was worshipped as the principal deity.
aten-c04 (historical-record): Akhenaten founded Amarna as a center for his beliefs.
aten-c05 (historical-record): Akhenaten's reform excluded the cult of other deities.
aten-c06 (historical-record): The Great Hymn to Aten is a major source for Akhenaten's religious reform.
aten-c07 (religious-tradition): In the Great Hymn, Aten is praised as the nurse in the womb who gives breath to cause all he has made to live.
aten-c08 (scholarly-interpretation): According to World History Encyclopedia, before Akhenaten's reform Aten was a minor solar deity.
aten-c09 (historical-record): After Akhenaten's death Tutankhamun moved the capital away from Akhetaten.
aten-c10 (historical-record): Tutankhamun began restoring Egypt's earlier religious practices.
aten-c11 (historical-record): The Amarna temples had open spaces and offering altars.
aten-c12 (historical-record): In his fifth year of reign, Amenhotep IV changed his birth name to Akhenaten, 'one who is akh for the Aten'.
aten-c13 (historical-record): The king founded a new city, Akhetaten 'Horizon of the Aten', at a desert bay halfway between Memphis and Thebes.
aten-c14 (historical-record): The Amarna hymn to the sun god is radically different in systematically excluding any other deities.
aten-c15 (religious-tradition): The Great Hymn addresses Aten as rising beautiful from the horizon on heaven, a living disk and origin of life.
aten-c16 (religious-tradition): According to the Great Hymn, Aten's rays bind the lands to the limit of all he has made.
aten-c17 (religious-tradition): The Great Hymn says Aten is distant yet his rays are on earth; he is in sight, but his movements are hidden.
aten-c18 (religious-tradition): In the Great Hymn Aten removes the darkness and grants his rays, and the two lands are in festival.
aten-c19 (religious-tradition): The Great Hymn praises Aten as the one who causes seed to grow in women, turns seed into people, and makes the son live in his mother's womb.
aten-c20 (religious-tradition): The Great Hymn calls Aten a unique god with none beside him, who moulds the earth to his wish alone.
aten-c21 (religious-tradition): The Great Hymn names a Flood in the sky for foreigners and a Flood from the underworld for Egypt.
aten-c22 (religious-tradition): The Great Hymn states that Aten makes the seasons to nurture all he made, with winter to cool them and heat.
aten-c23 (religious-tradition): According to the Great Hymn the land comes into being by Aten's action; beings live when he has shone and die when he rests.
aten-c24 (historical-record): The Great Hymn is inscribed in thirteen columns of hieroglyphs on the west wall of the courtier Ay at Amarna.
aten-c25 (scholarly-interpretation): Aten, also written Aton, Atonu, or Itn, was the focus of Atenism, the religious system formally established in ancient Egypt by the pharaoh Akhenaten.
aten-c26 (scholarly-interpretation): The worship of Aten and Akhenaten's rule are major identifying features of the Eighteenth Dynasty period called the Amarna Period (c. 1353-1336 BCE).
aten-c27 (etymology): The word Aten appears in the Old Kingdom as a noun meaning "disc", anything flat and circular; the sun was called the "disc of the day" where Ra was thought to reside.
aten-c28 (scholarly-interpretation): The Aten was the disc of the sun and originally an aspect of Ra, the sun god of traditional ancient Egyptian religion.
aten-c29 (scholarly-interpretation): Under Amenhotep III Aten was the preeminent creator deity of a pantheon; only under his successor did Aten become the only god acknowledged in state worship.
aten-c30 (scholarly-interpretation): During the reign of Amenhotep III, Aten was widely worshipped as a solar deity and depicted as a falcon-headed god like Ra.
aten-c31 (scholarly-interpretation): In Atenism, the Aten created all countries and people and cares for every creature.
aten-c32 (scholarly-interpretation): In inscriptions such as the Hymn to the Aten and the King, the Aten is shown caring for the people through Akhenaten, making the royal family intermediaries for his worship.
aten-c33 (historical-record): Unlike the traditional enclosed Egyptian temple, temples of the Aten were open and unroofed so that the sun's rays could enter.
aten-c34 (historical-record): Not long after Akhenaten's death, his successor Tutankhamun reopened the state temples to other gods and re-positioned Amun as the pre-eminent solar deity.
aten-c35 (scholarly-interpretation): Some see Akhenaten as practising an Aten monolatry since he did not deny other gods but simply refrained from worshipping them; other scholars call the religion henotheistic.
aten-c36 (scholarly-interpretation): According to World History Encyclopedia, Akhenaten instituted the first known monotheistic state religion in the world and, according to some, monotheism itself.


=== horus (lengkap-informasi, batch-033)
Horus | tier rich | deity | jenis dewa | egyptian-mythology
Ringkas: Horus is an ancient Egyptian falcon-linked sky god, son of Isis and Osiris, who defeated Set and was bound to kingship.
Kemampuan: flight — Imagined as a falcon, Horus could fly far from Ra and return with vital information. [horus-c16]
Kemampuan: null — Horus the Younger was known as protector of Egypt's royalty, avenger of wrongs, and defender of order. [horus-c11]
Kemampuan: null — Based on his battles with Set, Horus was regarded as a war god invoked by Egyptian rulers before battle and praised afterwards. [horus-c11]
horus-c01 (religious-tradition): Horus is an Egyptian sky god name primarily referring to Horus the Elder and Horus the Younger.
horus-c02 (historical-record): The son of Osiris and Isis was called Horus the Child and later known in Greek tradition as Harpocrates.
horus-c03 (religious-tradition): Horus, son of Isis and Osiris, defeated his uncle Set and restored order.
horus-c04 (etymology): Horus is the Latin form of Egyptian Hor, meaning 'the Distant One'.
horus-c05 (religious-tradition): Horus the Younger was associated mainly with the sun and also with the moon.
horus-c06 (religious-tradition): Horus later combined with Ra as Ra-Harahkhte, a falcon-headed sun god.
horus-c07 (religious-tradition): The falcon was seen as an incarnation of Horus, god of the sky and kingship.
horus-c08 (historical-record): Pharaoh Nectanebo II emphasized a close bond with Horus as god of kingship.
horus-c09 (religious-tradition): Horus the Elder is sibling of Osiris, Isis, Set, and Nephthys and is called Horus the Great.
horus-c10 (religious-tradition): The symbols of Horus are the Eye of Horus and the falcon.
horus-c11 (religious-tradition): Horus the Younger was protector of Egypt's royalty, avenger of wrongs, defender of order, uniter of the two lands, and a war god invoked by Egyptian rulers before battle.
horus-c12 (religious-tradition): In the account of Horus the Elder, Osiris governed the earth with Isis while Horus was given charge of the sky and specifically the sun.
horus-c13 (religious-tradition): The elder Horus is one of Egypt's oldest gods, born of the union of Geb (earth) and Nut (sky) shortly after the creation of the world.
horus-c14 (religious-tradition): In another version Horus is the son of Hathor; in others she is his wife, and sometimes she is mother, wife, and daughter of Horus.
horus-c15 (religious-tradition): Horus is also depicted as a creator god and benevolent protector.
horus-c16 (religious-tradition): Imagined as a falcon, Horus could fly far from Ra and return with vital information, and likewise quickly bring comfort to those in need.
horus-c17 (historical-record): From the Early Dynastic Period (c. 3150-c. 2613 BCE) onward Horus was linked with the king of Egypt, though later rulers associated themselves with Horus the Younger.
horus-c18 (religious-tradition): In the Osiris myth, before Osiris departed, Isis transformed herself into a kite and flew around his body, becoming pregnant with Horus.
horus-c19 (historical-record): The battles of Horus and Set exist in many versions; the best known is from a 20th Dynasty manuscript (1090-1077 BCE), The Contendings of Horus and Set, which presents the contest as a trial before the Ennead, a tribunal of nine gods.
horus-c20 (religious-tradition): In these battles Horus loses an eye and Set is castrated or at least severely damaged, but Horus wins each time.
horus-c21 (religious-tradition): In another version the trial lasts 80 years until the gods turn to Neith, mediator of disputes, who rules in favor of Horus.
horus-c22 (religious-tradition): In the first version Set is banished to the deserts beyond Egypt's borders while Horus takes his father's throne with his mother and aunt Nephthys as consorts.
horus-c23 (religious-tradition): Having conquered Set and restored order, Horus became known as Horu-Sema-Tawy, the Horus who unites the Two Lands.
horus-c24 (historical-record): For this reason Egyptian kings from the First Dynastic Period onward aligned themselves with Horus and chose a "Horus Name" at their coronation.
horus-c25 (religious-tradition): Horus was associated with the afterlife through his Four Sons, who protected the vital organs of the deceased.
horus-c26 (historical-record): In Upper Egypt Horus was worshipped with Hathor and their son Harsomptus at Edfu and Kom Ombos.
horus-c27 (historical-record): By the Ptolemaic Dynasty (323-30 BCE) the elder Horus had been completely replaced by the younger.
horus-c28 (scholarly-interpretation): Britannica 1911 calls Horus a solar divinity who appears very early in the Osiris cycle, a son of Isis and probably of Osiris, and opponent of Set.
horus-c29 (etymology): Britannica 1911 says the name Hōr is connected with a root meaning "upper" and probably means "the high-flyer".
horus-c30 (religious-tradition): In the judgment scene Horus introduces the deceased to Osiris.
horus-c31 (historical-record): To the Greeks Horus was equivalent to Apollo.
horus-c32 (religious-tradition): One epithet of Horus is Harendotes, "Hōr, avenger of his father (Osiris)".
horus-c33 (religious-tradition): Har-behtet, "Hōr of Beht", is the winged solar disk, god of Edfu (Apollinopolis Magna).
horus-c34 (religious-tradition): Isis gave birth to Horus alone in the Delta swamps after a difficult pregnancy and exceptionally long labor.
horus-c35 (historical-record): Rulers of Egypt's Predynastic Period (c. 6000-3150 BCE) were known as "Followers of Horus", attesting to even earlier veneration.
horus-c36 (scholarly-interpretation): According to Egyptologist Richard H. Wilkinson, Horus was one of the earliest Egyptian deities and his name is attested from the beginning of the Dynastic Period.
